FIELD NOTES

A universe of detail.
A matter of scale.

Orbital Atlas is a visual exploration, with data drawn from NASA’s planetary fact sheets. Each world is painted procedurally, so there are no external textures or asset downloads.

Read the scales

Illustrative sizes enlarge planets for visibility. Compressed distances bring the outer worlds closer. Neither preserves physical proportions.

True ratios preserve actual radii; True AU ratios preserve orbital separations. Switch both to true for one physical scale, where 1 astronomical unit = 22 scene units. Planets then become tiny in the overview; use Destinations to get close. Label markers stay visible and are not planet sizes.

A model, not a live ephemeris

Orbital periods, eccentricities, inclinations, and spin periods use approximate NASA values. Elliptical orbits use Kepler’s equation. Axial spin runs at 1/60 of orbital time for visual readability. Starting orbital phases are composed for this scene; positions are not a prediction of today’s sky. The Moon’s orbit is enlarged in illustrative mode. Trails show the previous 30° of each orbit, rather than a fixed time window.

Explore your way

Drag or swipe to rotate. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Arrow keys rotate the camera; + / − zoom. Space pauses time, R returns home, H hides the interface, and Escape closes a panel or ends the tour. Tour timing pauses when you leave the tab.
